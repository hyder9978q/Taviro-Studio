# Arabic AI-at-Work Evidence Pack — Pilot 01

**Research track:** Arabic
**Research case:** `case-arabic-ai-work-pilot`
**Status:** Evidence collection / pre-interpretation
**Research date:** 2026-09-18
**Scope:** Arabic-speaking / Middle East and Iraq work contexts

> This pack records attributable evidence only. Regional evidence is not treated as evidence about Iraq unless the source explicitly covers Iraq. Observations and interpretations are kept separate from source claims.

## Evidence records

### E001 — Middle East employee AI usage
- **Source:** PwC Middle East, *Workforce Hopes and Fears Survey 2025*
- **Published:** 2025-12-15
- **URL:** https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/issues/upskilling/hopes-and-fears-2025.html
- **Population/context:** 1,286 public- and private-sector employees across the Middle East.
- **Evidence:** 75% reported using AI in their jobs in the previous 12 months; 32% reported daily GenAI use; 82% said AI improved productivity.
- **Direct implication:** AI use in the regional workforce is already substantial; this is not a purely awareness-stage market.
- **Limitations:** Regional sample; not an Iraq-specific estimate; self-reported survey data.

### E002 — GCC workplace adoption and time savings
- **Source:** BCG, *From Pilots to Progress: AI at Work in the GCC*
- **Published:** 2025-09-25
- **URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-gcc-pilots-to-progress
- **Population/context:** More than 1,000 leaders, managers, and frontline employees across Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and UAE.
- **Evidence:** 87% reported using AI tools at work at least several times a week; more than half reported saving over an hour per day with AI; 81% said their companies were rolling out GenAI tools to foster productivity.
- **Evidence:** 45% reported satisfaction with AI training; 55% of frontline employees reported clear leadership guidance/support.
- **Limitations:** GCC only; not representative of Iraq or the wider Arabic-speaking population.

### E003 — Adoption/value gap in GCC organizations
- **Source:** McKinsey, *The state of AI in GCC countries: In pursuit of scale and value*
- **Published:** 2025-11-13
- **URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/middle-east/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-in-gcc-countries-in-pursuit-of-scale-and-value
- **Population/context:** 139 senior executives and board directors in GCC organizations; survey conducted Aug–Sep 2025; 14 executive interviews.
- **Evidence:** 84% of respondents said their organizations had adopted AI to some extent, but only 31% said their organizations had reached scaling/full deployment maturity; only 11% qualified as value realizers under the study definition.
- **Evidence:** Interviewees identified resistance to change as a major adoption barrier; the report states workflow redesign has a strong link to AI value creation.
- **Limitations:** Executive/board sample; GCC only; organizational adoption does not directly measure individual employee behavior.

### E004 — Iraqi institutional demand for AI-at-work skills
- **Source:** Ministry of Planning, Iraq, *Workshop on AI and digital transformation policies*
- **Published:** 2025-07-10
- **URL:** https://mop.gov.iq/archives/32047
- **Population/context:** Iraqi Ministry of Planning; institutional workplace context.
- **Evidence:** The ministry reported discussing the use of AI within ministry work processes, digitizing paper procedures, and developing the work environment using AI/digital technologies; the workshop specifically targeted employees and senior leadership.
- **Observation:** Iraqi public institutions are publicly discussing AI as a workplace/process-improvement capability.
- **Limitations:** Institutional announcement, not a workforce survey; does not establish adoption rates or user preferences.

### E005 — Iraqi training activity around AI and work
- **Source:** University of Kerbala, College of Computer Science and IT, *AI in the labor market: tools and software integration* workshop
- **Published:** 2025-05-05
- **URL:** https://csit.uokerbala.edu.iq/blog/%D9%83%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%88%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%88%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%AA%D9%83%D9%86%D9%88%D9%84%D9%88%D8%AC%D9%8A%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%88%D9%85-103/
- **Population/context:** Iraqi university / students and academic-industrial stakeholders.
- **Evidence:** The workshop covered practical AI tools including ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, DALL-E, and Power BI Copilot, API integration, and practical project ideas such as intelligent archiving, educational assistants, text analysis, and automated testing.
- **Observation:** AI-at-work discussion in Iraq includes concrete tools and integration use cases, not only abstract awareness.
- **Limitations:** Workshop participation is not a representative market sample.

### E006 — Iraqi media-worker field study
- **Source:** *Future use of AI in Iraqi satellite channels and its impact on workers: a field study*
- **Published/indexed:** 2025
- **URL:** https://msar.edu.iq/index.php/msar/article/view/107
- **Population/context:** 145 purposively selected workers knowledgeable about AI technology in Iraqi satellite channels.
- **Evidence:** The study reports limited AI adoption by Iraqi satellite channels and reports worker concerns that AI use could lead to job displacement; workers also perceived a need to develop their skills, tools, and knowledge.
- **Observation:** In at least one Iraqi occupational context, AI adoption coexists with both low organizational adoption and employment-related concern.
- **Limitations:** One sector; purposive sample; cannot be generalized to Iraqi workers overall.

### E007 — Arab-region employment outlook
- **Source:** ILO Regional Office for Arab States / ESCWA, *Artificial intelligence and employment prospects in the Arab region*
- **Published:** 2026
- **URL:** https://www.ilo.org/ar/publications/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B0%D9%83%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B5%D8%B7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%8A-%D9%88%D8%A2%D9%81%D8%A7%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%81-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B7%D9%82%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9
- **Population/context:** Arab region; strategic foresight for 2025–2035.
- **Evidence:** The report examines how AI may reshape employment across sectors and occupations and highlights distributional risks, including exclusion of older workers, persons with disabilities, migrants, and refugees.
- **Use in Taviro:** Context for the employment environment, not evidence of a specific audience's preferences or behavior.
- **Limitations:** Foresight report; not a direct audience-behavior survey.

## Evidence-derived observations

### O001
Regional employee AI usage is already substantial in the Middle East, while GCC organizational maturity/value capture remains materially lower than raw adoption figures.

**Evidence:** E001, E002, E003.

### O002
In Iraq, public institutional activity and educational activity are explicitly connecting AI with workplace process improvement, skills, and labor-market adaptation.

**Evidence:** E004, E005.

### O003
At least one Iraqi occupational field study reports a combination of limited organizational adoption, worker concern about job displacement, and perceived need for skill development.

**Evidence:** E006.

### O004
The available evidence supports investigating the transition from isolated AI use/tool experimentation to repeatable workflow integration as a research problem.

**Evidence:** E001, E002, E003; this is an observation about the research gap, not a claim that all Arabic users experience it.

## Candidate interpretations — NOT YET APPROVED

### I001
A potentially important audience need is not basic AI awareness, but practical translation of AI capability into reliable work processes.

**Supporting evidence:** E002, E003, E004, E005.
**Confidence:** Medium.
**Alternative explanation:** The apparent gap may partly reflect organizational governance, training, infrastructure, or leadership rather than individual user difficulty.

### I002
Job security and employability may interact with AI adoption motivation among some Arabic-speaking workers.

**Supporting evidence:** E001 and E006.
**Confidence:** Low–Medium.
**Alternative explanation:** Concerns may be occupation-specific or driven by broader labor-market uncertainty rather than AI itself.

## Candidate hypotheses for experiment design

### H001
Among Arabic-speaking employees and business operators who already know about generative AI, content demonstrating a concrete end-to-end workflow may generate more meaningful engagement than generic AI education.

**Status:** Untested.
**Evidence chain:** E001/E002/E003 → O001/O004 → I001 → H001.
**Required validation:** behavioral content experiment plus audience-specific qualitative research.

### H002
Content framed around saving time and reducing repetitive work may attract people who already use AI but have not integrated it into a repeatable workflow.

**Status:** Untested.
**Evidence chain:** E002/E003 → O001/O004 → I001 → H002.
**Required validation:** controlled content test; do not assume this framing is culturally dominant.

### H003
In Iraq, perceived job displacement and the need to develop practical skills may coexist, creating demand for AI content framed around capability-building rather than replacement.

**Status:** Untested.
**Evidence chain:** E006 → O003 → I002 → H003.
**Required validation:** additional Iraqi occupational/community evidence before content decisions.

## Research gaps

1. No representative Iraq-wide survey of AI-at-work behavior has been established in this pack.
2. No reliable segmentation by Iraqi occupation/business type yet exists.
3. No direct measurement yet connects specific pain points to content engagement or conversion.
4. No systematic Arabic social-community ethnography has yet been completed.
5. No evidence yet establishes which Arabic dialect/register performs best for the target audience.
6. No evidence yet establishes whether employees and business owners should remain one segment or split into separate research tracks.
7. No evidence yet establishes which platform should be the primary acquisition channel.

## Immediate research actions

1. Build separate Iraqi employee and Iraqi business-operator field maps.
2. Collect attributable public digital observations from relevant Arabic communities/platforms under ethical and privacy constraints.
3. Sample multiple occupational contexts rather than treating "Arabic workers" as one homogeneous group.
4. Triangulate public digital observations with published surveys/research and, where possible, direct interviews or questionnaires.
5. Only promote interpretations to approved hypotheses after evidence convergence.
6. Feed validated hypotheses into Taviro's existing idea-scoring/brief pipeline and test behavior experimentally.

## Research integrity rules

- Regional/GCC evidence must not be represented as Iraqi evidence.
- Individual posts/comments are observations, not population estimates.
- Public availability does not imply unrestricted reuse.
- No sensitive-attribute inference.
- No invented quotations, sources, dates, or sample characteristics.
- Competing explanations remain attached to interpretations.
- Confidence describes evidence strength, not certainty about people.
